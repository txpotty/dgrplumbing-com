    Hidden Wall and Ceiling Leaks in Fayetteville

    Bubbling paint, soft drywall and a musty smell come up on most of our calls. All of them mean water. A thermal scan shows the wet patch, and a moisture meter confirms it. Acoustic tools help when the leak is on a pressurized line, and once the source is pinned down we cut a single neat access opening instead of chasing the stain across the wall.

    Sewer Line Leak Detection in Fayetteville, AR

    A cracked drain line leaks wastewater into the soil, which can show up as a sewer smell in the yard, a soggy patch or slow drains. We use a camera for these. Our tech runs a camera from the cleanout to the city tap, recording cracks, offset joints and root entry in PVC, clay and cast iron, so you see the exact distance and depth of the break.

    PEX and Copper Leak Detection in Fayetteville, AR

    On a ground microphone, copper leaks are easy to hear. PEX is quieter, since plastic pipe does not carry sound well, so we lean more on thermal scans, moisture readings and tracer gas when the house is plumbed in it. We handle both every week. Pinholes in copper and failed crimp fittings in PEX are the two leaks we find most.

    Leaks in Walls and Ceilings in Fayetteville, AR

    A brown ring on the ceiling under a bathroom usually points to a shower valve, a drain joint or a toilet wax ring. We figure out which. Thermal cameras show the cool wet area, a moisture meter confirms it and a borescope through a small hole lets us see the actual pipe before any big opening is made.

    Slab Leak Detection in Fayetteville

    Concrete slab homes have supply lines buried in sand below the floor, and a pinhole there can run for months before anyone notices. A warm tile is the classic sign. We pressure test the hot and cold sides separately, then use a ground microphone to hear the leak through the concrete and confirm with a thermal camera when the leak is on the hot side.
