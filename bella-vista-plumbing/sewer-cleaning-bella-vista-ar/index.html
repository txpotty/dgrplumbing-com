    Dealing with Grease Buildup in Bella Vista, AR

    Grease hardens as it cools. Then it narrows the pipe. Kitchen grease coats the sewer line the same way plaque coats an artery, and over a few years the opening can close to the size of a finger, which is when drains start to back up, and jetting is the right fix.

    Sewer Cleaning Versus Sewer Repair in Bella Vista, AR

    Cleaning cannot fix a broken pipe. It only removes what is inside. If your sewer backs up every few months, the camera usually shows a belly, a crack or a collapsed section, and we compare the yearly cost of cleanings to a spot repair, a liner or a replacement, with the numbers in writing.

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Lines in Bella Vista

    Older properties may still have clay or Orangeburg pipe. Both have weak spots. Clay joints invite roots, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid-1900s, flattens under the weight of the soil over time, so we clean these lines carefully and map the joints and soft spots on camera.

    Jetting a Sewer Line in Bella Vista, AR

    Grease and scale are jetting work. Cables leave them behind. A jetter nozzle with rear-facing jets pulls itself down the line and blasts the buildup off the pipe wall, restoring close to the full diameter of the pipe and slowing the next clog.

    Root Removal From Sewer Lines in Bella Vista

    Trees send roots toward any moisture they can find. Leaky joints feed them all year. Our root saw cuts the mass loose, and heavy growth gets a jetter pass to clean out the fine hairs, and a camera shows where roots are entering so you can choose between lining and repair.

    Tree Root Removal in Bella Vista, AR Sewers

    Roots get in through tiny gaps. Then they fill the pipe. A root saw on a sectional cable cuts the mass loose so we can pull it out through the cleanout, and a jetter can follow to flush out the fine root hairs left on the pipe wall, and a camera shows where they are getting in.

    Sewer Camera Inspections in Bella Vista, AR

    On a repeat backup, the camera goes in first. We would rather look than guess. A waterproof color camera on a push rod shows the inside of the line live on a screen, so we can see the clog, measure its distance from the cleanout and spot any crack or sag.
