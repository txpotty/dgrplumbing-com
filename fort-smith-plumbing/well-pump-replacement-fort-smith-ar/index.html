    Submersible and Jet Pump Replacement in Fort Smith, AR

    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Fort Smith

    Sand kills submersibles early. Grit in a well chews up impellers fast, so we look for it on the old pump and may set the new one higher or add a sand separator. Pump setting depth matters.

    Drop Pipe and Wire Replacement in Fort Smith

    Galvanized drop pipe from the 1970s and 1980s rusts from the inside, and a pinhole at 150 feet will bleed pressure every time the pump shuts off. We replace it with schedule 120 PVC or poly pipe, depending on the depth and the pump weight. Wire gets the same look. Nicked jackets and old tape splices go.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Fort Smith

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes a healthy pump look sick. If the pump clicks on every time someone runs a faucet for ten seconds, the bladder in the tank has probably failed and the pump is short cycling itself to death. A tire gauge on the air valve tells us a lot. Precharge should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure.

    What a Well Pump Job Costs in Fort Smith, AR

    A well pump job usually runs a few thousand dollars, and the pump itself is often less than half of that. The rest is labor, wire, pipe and fittings, plus the hoist time to get it all down the hole. You see every line item.

    Jet Pump Seal and Motor Replacement in Fort Smith, AR

    Single-stage and two-stage jet pumps wear out at the seal and impeller, and a leaking shaft seal shows up as a puddle under the motor. New pump, primed and tested. Then we watch the gauge through a full cycle.

    Grundfos Well Pump Replacement in Fort Smith, AR

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have a built-in soft start and dry-run protection, and they run on two wires with no control box at the surface. They are pricier than a standard pump. Add the CU 301 controller to an SQE and you get constant pressure.

    Agricultural and Ranch Well Pumps in Fort Smith

    Irrigation and stock wells often run 3 to 10 HP pumps, sometimes on three-phase power with a motor starter and overload protection at the panel. We replace those pumps and the starters that protect them. Timing matters on a ranch.

    Well Pump Sizing in Fort Smith

    Sizing starts with the well log. We get depth and static level from the well log or a sounder, then a draw-down test shows what the well can really produce in gallons per minute. Pumps bigger than the well can supply run dry.
