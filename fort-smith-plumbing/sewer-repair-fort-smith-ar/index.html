    Emergency Sewer Repair in Fort Smith

    Raw sewage on a basement floor is not a next-week problem. We answer at night and on weekends. The first goal on an emergency call is to get the line flowing, usually with a cable or jetter, and then camera it to find out whether you have a clog or a structural failure that will back up again tomorrow. The real repair may be scheduled for the next morning. Until then, we tell you which fixtures are safe to use.

    Commercial Sewer Repair in Fort Smith

    Multifamily buildings and offices put several units' worth of flow through one lateral. Small defects turn into big backups. We repair larger diameter lines, add two-way cleanouts so future maintenance is easy, and give managers recorded footage for owners, insurers and their own files. We can work outside business hours.

    Tree Root Invasion Solutions in Fort Smith

    Roots in a sewer line are not the tree's fault. The pipe was leaking first. Warm, wet, nutrient-rich air escaping from a loose joint draws roots toward the line, and once they get in they thicken until they crack the pipe or separate the joint completely. We cut them out, then talk about the long-term fix. A liner or new pipe at that joint is what actually stops regrowth.

    Bellied Sewer Line Correction in Fort Smith, AR

    A belly is a low spot where the pipe has sagged and holds standing water. Waste collects in the dip. Rocky, shallow soil in hilly parts of Arkansas makes trenching harder and can raise the cost on sewer and water line replacements. We measure the depth of standing water on camera, dig out that section, re-bed the pipe on compacted gravel and reset the slope to the quarter inch per foot most codes call for on residential laterals. Not every sag needs digging, and we will tell you which yours is.

    Sewer Line Leak Detection & Repair in Fort Smith, AR

    Sewer smell outside the house points to a leak. So does an extra-green strip of lawn. We confirm it on camera, look for cracks, open joints or a broken cleanout riser, and locate it on the surface to keep the excavation tight. A small crack can sometimes be fixed with a short sectional liner, with no digging at all. Larger breaks get cut out and replaced.

    Cracked and Broken Pipe Repair in Fort Smith

    Clay and cast iron crack from settling soil, tree roots and vehicles parked over the line. Dirt falls in through the crack. We excavate at the marked spot, cut out the damaged length and install new Schedule 40 PVC with shielded stainless couplings rated for the transition between materials, then run water through it and check it on camera before backfill. Most of these are one-day jobs.

    Pipe Burst & Collapse Repair in Fort Smith, AR

    A collapsed sewer pipe stops all flow at the point of failure. Every drain backs up. Collapses usually come from corroded cast iron, crushed clay under a driveway or tar-paper pipe that has finally flattened, and the camera tells us exactly where the pipe gave way and how deep it sits. The broken length comes out and new pipe goes in. Lining cannot fix a full collapse.

    Sewer Repair for Commercial Properties in Fort Smith, AR

Grease trap outlet lines are one of our most common commercial repairs.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Repair in Fort Smith

    A single spot repair typically runs $1,500 to $4,500. Depth drives most of that. The written quote follows the camera inspection.

    Most spot repairs are done in one day, excavation and backfill included. Some take longer. Larger replacements, or jobs that need a right-of-way permit to dig near the street, usually run two to four days with inspection timing as the main variable. We put the schedule in writing.

    Flush only toilet paper. Flushable wipes are not.     A rental auger can open a soft clog. Structural damage stays. Forcing a cable through a broken or offset joint can snag, tear the pipe further or leave a cutter head stuck underground, and then someone has to dig to get the cable out. Repeat clogs deserve a video inspection.

    A spot repair or a jetting job usually takes a few hours. Lining takes a day or two. A full replacement with excavation can take two to five days, depending on the length of the run, the depth of the pipe and how long the inspection takes to schedule. The schedule is in the written estimate.

    Schedule 40 PVC is our standard for open trench work. PVC will not corrode. For pipe bursting we use fused HDPE, which comes in continuous lengths with no joints for roots to find, while lining jobs use epoxy-saturated tubes built for long service life. Local code decides the final choice.
