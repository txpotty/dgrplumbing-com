    Water chemistry changes from one street to the next, and so does anode life. Most rods last three to five years. A softened water supply can chew through a magnesium rod in two. A new rod costs a fraction of a tank and often adds years.

    Gas Venting Tune-Up in Fort Smith, AR

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. We check draft at the hood. On newer FVIR models, garage dust and dryer lint can clog the flame arrestor screen at the bottom, which starves the burner and makes the pilot drop out. We vacuum it clean.

    Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Fort Smith, AR

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. On gas units we inspect the burner, thermocouple and vent connector. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Fort Smith

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. The new valve must match the tank's BTU or kilowatt rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Burner and Pilot Service in Fort Smith, AR

    On a gas tank, a dirty burner throws a lazy yellow flame and leaves soot on the flue baffle. The burner comes out and gets brushed and vacuumed. We also check the thermocouple or flame sensor, since a weak one lets the pilot go out every few days and leaves you resetting it on your knees. The goal is a steady blue flame.

    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Fort Smith
