    Trenchless water line repair is a method of fixing or replacing underground pipes without extensive digging. It's an great option for preserving landscaping and minimizing disruption, especially on properties in Fort Smith with established gardens or driveways.

    Common signs include a wet or unusually green patch of yard, a hissing sound near the meter, low pressure at every fixture and a water bill that climbs without explanation. Dirty or rusty water can also signal a damaged line. If your meter moves with all fixtures off, you likely have a leak.

    Rural wells in Arkansas often carry iron, sediment, or a sulfur odor, which can stain fixtures and foul aerators and water heaters.     Water lines can leak or break due to several factors, including aging pipes (corrosion in galvanized steel or copper), shifting soil, tree root intrusion, high water pressure, ground freezing and thawing cycles, and accidental damage during excavation.     Common signs of a broken water line include unusually high water bills, a sudden drop in water pressure, wet spots in your yard or on pavement that don't dry, discolored water, or the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use.
