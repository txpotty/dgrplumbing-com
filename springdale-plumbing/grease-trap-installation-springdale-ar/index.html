    What Goes Into a Grease Trap Installation in Springdale, AR

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Installation in Springdale

    Outdoor gravity interceptors run from 750 gallons to well past 2,000, and setting one means an excavator on site and a traffic-rated lid if it sits in the drive lane. Before anything ships, we check that the tank and covers carry an H-20 traffic rating where trucks will roll over them. Most go in over two to four days. The inlet invert has to sit a couple of inches above the outlet, and we shoot both with a laser level before backfill.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Springdale, AR

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. You can put one under a sink, beside a dish station or in a pit in the slab. The flow control is not optional. Without it, a dumped sink pushes water through faster than the unit is rated for and the grease goes out the outlet. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    Grease Trap Installation Cost in Springdale, AR

    Price follows size and location more than brand. The low end is a small indoor unit with easy access and a short pipe run. Outdoor tanks with digging and a permit sit at the top of the range. We quote after a site visit.

    Zurn Grease Trap Installation in Springdale

    Zurn's indoor grease interceptors cover the common sizes from small under-sink units up to 100 GPM, and we set enough of them to know where the gaskets give trouble. After a few years of hot water and scraping, the lid gasket is what usually leaks. We keep spares on the truck. Most Zurn units come with a flow control fitting, and we mount it on the inlet side at the height the instructions call for.

    In-Ground Gravity Interceptor Installation in Springdale, AR

    Buffets, grocery delis and kitchens doing several hundred covers a night usually end up with an in-ground gravity interceptor of 1,000 gallons or more. The work is excavation and a level gravel bed, then the tank and its inlet and outlet piping. Rocky, shallow soil in hilly parts of Arkansas makes trenching harder and can add time on sewer and water line replacements. We plan the dig and the backfill around what the soil report and the open hole actually show us. Risers bring the lids to grade.

    Emergency Grease Trap Services in Springdale, AR

    A grease trap backing up into the floor sinks at the start of Friday dinner service means a closed kitchen and a health inspector who may not let you reopen until it is fixed. First job is figuring out whether the clog is in the trap or the pipe on either side of it. Often it is the outlet. If the trap itself has failed, we can usually set a temporary unit and schedule the permanent replacement.

    New Restaurant Build-Out Grease Traps in Springdale

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. The drawings and the equipment list from your kitchen designer tell us what will drain where. Changes get expensive. Moving a trap after the concrete cures means cutting the floor, so we catch problems while it is still paper.

    Residential Grease Interceptor Setup in Springdale, AR

    Homes rarely need a grease trap, though some do. Licensed home bakeries and houses on old clay laterals that keep clogging are the usual cases. A compact under-sink unit handles the load, and a homeowner can clean it in a few minutes. Check with your local health office first.
