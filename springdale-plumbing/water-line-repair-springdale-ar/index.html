    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Springdale

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. The new line travels between them without a trench. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, and most jobs wrap up in a single day.

    Commercial Water Line Repair in Springdale, AR

    Commercial service lines are often 1 1/2 to 4 inch pipe feeding a meter bank, a backflow assembly and sometimes a fire line. A break shuts the business down. DGR Plumbing can schedule repairs early in the morning or after closing, with large-diameter couplings and fittings already loaded. We also arrange the backflow test and inspection that most water providers require after work on the commercial side of the meter.

    PEX Water Line Repair in Springdale, AR

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rodents chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Copper Water Line Repair in Springdale

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Rocks pressing against the pipe wear pinholes over time, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. We usually cut out two feet. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, and we check the exposed pipe on both sides for green crust or soft spots.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Springdale

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Newer homes across Arkansas mostly use PEX and PVC, which resist corrosion but still benefit from proper support and freeze protection. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. You get a price on every option.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Springdale, AR

    Replacement beats repair once a line has leaked two or three times, or when it is galvanized or polybutylene. Patches on those materials rarely hold. We run new Type K copper or PEX from the meter to the house, typically finishing in one day whether we trench or pull the pipe. We pull the permit, set up the inspection and pressure test the new line before it is covered.

    Commercial Water Line Services in Springdale, AR

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, rusty water and a climbing bill are other common clues. The surest check is your meter. If it moves with everything off, you have a leak.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. A full replacement takes about a day.     Fix small leaks early. A drip becomes a gusher. Check your pressure once a year, because a bad regulator stresses every pipe in the house, and keep big-rooted trees off the pipe route.     You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. Buried repairs are not a weekend project. They need the leak located, 811 marking, safe digging around the gas and power lines nearby and code-approved fittings made up correctly. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.

    Most jurisdictions in Arkansas require a permit for a full service line replacement. Spot repairs vary. The permit usually comes from the city or county building department, with an inspection of the exposed pipe and bedding before we cover it. DGR Plumbing handles that paperwork for you.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, from locating the leak to filling the hole. Replacements take longer.
