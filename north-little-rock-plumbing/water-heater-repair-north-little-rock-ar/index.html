    Anode Rod Replacement in North Little Rock, AR

    The anode rod sacrifices itself so the steel tank does not rust. In hard water it can be eaten away within a few years. We pull and inspect the rod, then install a magnesium, aluminum or powered anode depending on your water and any odor problems. Rotten-egg odor often clears up.

    Water Heater Brands We Service in North Little Rock

    Rheem, Bradford White, State and American Standard are the tank brands we see most, with Navien and Rinnai leading on tankless. Each brand has its own weak spots.     Tankless Water Heater Repair in North Little Rock

    Scale is the main enemy of a tankless heater. Mineral buildup on the heat exchanger triggers overheating codes and shutdowns. We descale with a pump and approved solution, clean the inlet filter and check the igniter and flow sensor while the unit is open. All major brands.

    Gas Burner and Venting Repair in North Little Rock, AR

    Combustion is the part of a gas heater that matters most for safety. Yellow flame tips or soot at the draft hood mean the burner is not getting enough air to burn cleanly. We clean the burner and check draft with a mirror or a smoke test. Backdrafting is dangerous.

    Gas Water Heater Repair in North Little Rock, AR

    Gas units add a burner, a pilot or igniter and a gas control valve to the list of parts that can fail. Most no-heat calls trace to the thermocouple or the gas control. We leak test each gas connection after the repair. Draft at the vent gets checked too.

    Leaking Water Heater Repair in North Little Rock, AR

    Not every leak means a dead tank. Relief valve, drain valve and supply line drips are cheap fixes. A leak from the tank body or the bottom seam usually means the inner lining has failed, and that tank needs to be replaced soon. We protect your floors either way.

    Electric Tank Repair in North Little Rock, AR

    Four parts can fail on an electric tank, two elements and two thermostats. Hard water piles scale on the lower element until it burns out. We test resistance and voltage, replace the failed part and reset the high limit, then watch a full heating cycle before we call it done. A breaker that keeps tripping gets traced first.
