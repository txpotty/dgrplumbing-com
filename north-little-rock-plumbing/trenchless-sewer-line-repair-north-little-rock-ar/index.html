    Sewer Lining and Pipe Bursting in North Little Rock, AR

    Commercial Trenchless Sewer Repair in North Little Rock, AR

    Office and retail buildings often have a cast iron building drain under the slab that has rotted along the bottom. Lining it avoids cutting the floor. Work gets scheduled around tenant hours, nights included. A typical commercial lining job runs one to three days, depending on footage and how many branch connections need to be reinstated with a cutter.

    Sectional Point Repair in North Little Rock

    A point repair is a short liner, often 3 to 5 feet long, set over one bad spot. We push it in on a packer, guided by the camera footage, and the packer inflates to press the resin-soaked sleeve against the pipe wall until it cures. It fixes a single crack or root entry, not a whole failing line. Sound lines with one bad joint are the right candidates.

    Pipe Bursting in North Little Rock, AR

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. Before pulling, the crew heat-fuses sections of HDPE into one continuous length on the ground. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. It goes quickly.

    Trenchless vs Traditional Sewer Repair Cost in North Little Rock, AR

    Point repairs often cost $1,500 to $3,500, while full lining of a residential lateral commonly runs $4,000 to $12,000. Pipe bursting typically ranges from $5,000 to $15,000. On a short, shallow run, a traditional dig can come in lower. Once you add back concrete and sod, the gap usually closes, and we price both options side by side so you can see the difference.

    Trenchless Drain Pipe Lining in North Little Rock, AR

    Smaller drain lines inside the house can be lined too, including 2-inch kitchen drains and the 3-inch cast iron stacks found in many older homes.     Cast Iron Sewer Line Lining in North Little Rock, AR

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. Plumbers call that channel rot, a groove that eats through the bottom. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.
