    Foundation, Shower and Block Wall Waterproofing in North Little Rock, AR

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in North Little Rock, AR

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head spraying the wall instead of the plants, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. Then we clean the wall, let it dry and apply a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in North Little Rock, AR

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer with a line to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box. Water heaters get a pan too. Behind the heater we can install moisture-resistant board and seal it.

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in North Little Rock

    Older homes in Arkansas often still have galvanized supply pipe and cast iron drains, both of which corrode from the inside with age. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with steady hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and install drain tile where water pools along the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Some basements never need a sump.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in North Little Rock, AR

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized for the inflow, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed at least 10 feet from the house.

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in North Little Rock, AR

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and run it to a sump basin so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. It just stops puddling on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and works where a driveway or deck blocks the wall.

    Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in North Little Rock, AR

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the level and wait 24 hours. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a clamping drain tightened onto the liner. The new pan gets its own flood test before tile.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in North Little Rock

    Shower wall waterproofing with a membrane system usually runs $1,200 to $3,500 including demolition and backer board, but not counting the new tile. Block wall sealing outside often costs $800 to $3,000. Foundation waterproofing below grade is usually $2,500 to $8,000.

    A shower wall job usually takes two to four days from demolition to finished membrane, counting the drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing often wraps up in a day. Larger foundation jobs can run several days, and weather can stretch that.

    Schluter Kerdi, Custom RedGard and Laticrete Hydro Ban cover most of our showers. Sheet membranes like Kerdi suit tight schedules because there is nothing to cure, while liquids like RedGard and Hydro Ban go on in two coats and need to dry between them. We follow each maker's steps exactly.

    Always. Sealing a wall with an active leak inside it traps the water, and the framing rots faster than it would have if you had left it open. The leak gets repaired and the cavity dried before anything else.

    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before any sealer goes on, and for larger growth, roughly anything over ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. We remove smaller affected areas ourselves.

    Crack repairs take a few hours. Interior drain tile usually takes one to three days, and a full exterior dig around the whole house can run three to seven days or more depending on the weather and the depth of the footing. Your timeline comes with the quote.

    Xypex, Tremco, Sika and Henry products show up on a lot of our jobs, covering crystalline products, elastomeric membranes and sealants. The product depends on the wall. Poured concrete takes crystalline coatings well, while block often needs an elastomeric membrane or a drainage board.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, digging equipment, proper membrane thickness and knowing how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. A bad install can trap water against the wall and make things worse.
