    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in North Little Rock

    Well pump replacement usually costs $1,800 to $4,500 depending on well depth and pump size. Deep wells or constant pressure systems can cost $4,000 to $8,000.

    Regular maintenance, such as checking your well's water level, keeping the pressure tank air charge correct and fixing any leaks promptly, can extend your pump's life. Avoiding heavy water use and installing a good quality sediment filter can also reduce wear and tear on the pump.

    Well pump replacement is a complex job that involves high voltage electricity, heavy equipment and possibly deep well access. It requires specialized tools and experience for proper installation, electrical safety and compliance with local plumbing codes. It is strongly recommended to hire a licensed professional to avoid injury, property damage or costly mistakes.

    It is often a good idea to consider replacing your pressure tank at the same time as your well pump, especially if both are of similar age. A new pressure tank can improve the efficiency and lifespan of your new pump by reducing cycling. It also cuts labor costs to do both jobs at once.

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up from shallower wells, usually less than 25 feet. Submersible pumps are placed directly inside the well casing, submerged in water, and are used for deeper wells, pushing water up to the surface. Each has specific applications and benefits.

    Most new well pumps come with a manufacturer's warranty, typically ranging from 1 to 5 years, covering defects in materials and workmanship. DGR Plumbing also backs our installation work with our own labor warranty for complete peace of mind.

    DGR Plumbing installs and services a wide range of trusted well pump brands known for their durability and performance. These commonly include Grundfos, Franklin Electric, Goulds Pumps and Sta-Rite. We can recommend the best brand and model to suit your well's needs.

    Common signs of a failing well pump include no water, sputtering faucets, reduced water pressure, discolored water or surprisingly high electricity bills. If your pump runs constantly or cycles on and off too frequently, these are also signs that replacement might be necessary.
