    Replacing an Old Disposal in Jonesboro, AR

    Old disposals usually come off with a twist of the mounting ring, once the power is off and the dishwasher hose and discharge tube are disconnected. If the new unit is the same brand and mount style, the swap is faster, but we still replace the flange gasket and putty when the old seal has hardened. Seals harden with age. We haul the old unit away.

    Standard Disposal Installation in Jonesboro

    Most households in Jonesboro do well with a 1/3 to 1/2 horsepower continuous-feed unit. We remove the old unit if there is one, set a new flange in fresh plumber's putty, hang the disposal on a new mount and connect it to the trap with a new discharge tube and gasket. Wiring and testing come last. A smooth spin and a clean drain tell us it is right.

    Garbage Disposal Installation Cost Factors in Jonesboro, AR

    The model and the power setup drive most of the cost. A simple swap on a sink that already has a switched outlet is the least expensive job, while adding power, lowering the drain or reworking a double-bowl layout adds time and shows up as its own line on the quote. Unit and labor are quoted together.

    Disposal Installation for Double Sinks in Jonesboro, AR

    Double bowl sinks put the disposal on one side and a continuous waste or end outlet tee on the other. The layout keeps both bowls draining and stops the disposal from pushing water into the second bowl, a problem when the tee is too close to the discharge. We use heavy-wall fittings.

    High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in Jonesboro

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit takes up more room and weighs more than a basic unit, so we measure the cabinet first. Taller units can push the discharge outlet below the wall stub, and when that happens we either lower the drain at the wall or pick a model with a higher outlet so water does not sit in the chamber. Power needs a check too. Bigger motors draw more at startup.

    Commercial Disposal Setup in Jonesboro

    Restaurants and commercial kitchens in Jonesboro need disposals built to run all day. These units usually require a dedicated circuit, a larger drain connection and a control panel that runs water automatically, and some sewer districts limit or ban them, so we check first. Downtime costs money.

    Batch-Feed Units for Kitchens Without a Switch in Jonesboro

    Batch-feed disposals only run when the stopper lid is in place, which adds safety for families with kids. Because they need no wall switch, they suit kitchens where a new switch would mean cutting drywall or a tile backsplash. The drain hookup is the usual setup.

    Dishwasher Connection With Disposal Installation in Jonesboro

    New disposals come with a knockout plug in the dishwasher inlet. We knock it out with a screwdriver and hammer, fish the plug out of the chamber and connect the hose with a new clamp. A plug left in backs up the dishwasher. An air gap or high loop goes in too, which keeps sink water from running back into the dishwasher.
