    Full Home PEX Repiping in Jonesboro

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside out. Give it four or five decades and the inside is so packed with rust that a half-inch pipe barely flows, and the first draw in the morning runs brown. We replace every foot of it with PEX. You get real pressure back, and the discolored water goes away with the old pipe.

    PEX for Water Line Replacement in Jonesboro

    Your service line runs from the meter or well to the house, often a foot and a half to three feet underground. Because PEX comes on a long coil, the whole run from the meter to the foundation can go in with zero buried fittings. Homes here are split between crawlspace and slab construction, and crawlspaces allow more direct access for repairs and repiping. We bed it in sand. Rock pressing on the pipe is the usual cause of an early failure.

    PEX Main Water Line Installation in Jonesboro

    The main from the meter to the house is the one line everything else depends on. A leaking old service line shows up as a soggy patch in the yard, a higher water bill and weak pressure everywhere. We trench and lay new PEX. At the house we add a new full-port ball valve for the main shutoff.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Jonesboro

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A makes tighter bends, can be heat-fixed if it kinks, and its fittings flow better, while PEX-B is cheaper and its crimps hold just as well when each one is gauged. Either one lasts for decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Jonesboro

    Pinhole leaks in copper tend to come in clusters. Aggressive water, fast flow and soil contact under slabs can pit copper from the inside, and the first few pinholes are typically followed by more. Rural wells in Arkansas often carry iron, sediment, or a sulfur odor, which can stain fixtures and clog aerators and water heaters. Converting to PEX stops the cycle. Where new PEX meets existing copper stubs or valves, we use listed transition fittings.

    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Jonesboro, AR

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A bends the easiest and uses expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and uses crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C handles more like B. All three are code-listed for drinking water. We match the pipe to the fittings and tools from one maker so the warranty stays intact.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Jonesboro, AR

    Gray polybutylene was installed widely from the late seventies into the mid nineties. Chlorinated water breaks the pipe and its plastic fittings down from within, which means it can look perfectly fine until a fitting suddenly fails. Many insurers ask whether you have it. Swapping it for PEX can help with insurance and with resale.
