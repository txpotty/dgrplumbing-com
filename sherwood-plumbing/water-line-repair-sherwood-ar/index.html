    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Sherwood, AR

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Two or more leaks on galvanized is a replacement. Age matters too, and a line past about 40 years is usually near the end no matter the material. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. You see the cut-out section yourself.

    PEX Water Line Repair in Sherwood, AR

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rodents chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and sunlight exposure before burial weakens the plastic over time. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Sherwood

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. The deeper the pipe, the higher the price. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, while trenchless replacement tends to run $3,000 to $7,000 since it needs a boring rig or puller. The written price comes before we start.

    For PEX we use Uponor and Viega, depending on the job. Buried copper is always Type K. Couplings and transition fittings come from makers like Mueller and Ford, and we stick with fittings rated for direct burial in the soil conditions we find. Off-brand fittings stay off the truck.

    Keep trees and shrubs with big roots well away from the line. Mark the pipe route somewhere. Before a deep freeze, disconnect hoses, drip a faucet on outside walls, and know where your main shutoff is located. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you if the regulator is failing, and anything over 80 psi is worth fixing before it starts wearing on the line. On galvanized lines, budgeting for a new line is cheaper than an emergency.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. A full replacement takes about a day. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull sometimes run two or three days when concrete has to be cut and patched or the inspector cannot come right away. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Small repairs often do not. The rules come from your city or county, and they can differ even between neighboring towns in the same county. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. First, a cable is threaded through. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, while the new pipe is pulled into the path it just opened. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, and most home service lines qualify.

    Watch the bill first. An unexplained spike usually comes before anything you can see. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, rusty water and a climbing bill are other common clues. The surest check is your meter. If it moves with everything off, you have a leak.
