    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. The trouble spots are flange height and bolt torque. A flange that sits below the finished floor needs an extender or a thicker seal, and a nut tightened one turn too far can crack the base, so on new floors and wall-hung toilets a pro install is the safer bet.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Most homes measure 12 inches. You will also see 10 and 14 inch rough-ins, and buying the wrong one means the toilet will not fit or sits well off the wall. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    It is a toilet with the seat about 17 to 19 inches off the floor, roughly chair height, compared with 15 inches or so on a standard bowl. Most adults find it easier on the knees. Kids and shorter adults sometimes find their feet do not rest flat, so in a shared bathroom we sometimes suggest a standard bowl with a grab bar instead.

    Watch the base and the tank. Water that reappears on the floor after you wipe it up points to a cracked bowl or a failed seal. A seal can be replaced for far less than a new toilet, so we check that first, but a crack in the china means replacement, since there is no lasting repair for cracked porcelain.

    Plan on one to three hours for a straight swap. Setting the bowl itself is the quick part. Jobs that need a new flange or a patch of rotted subfloor can stretch toward half a day, and we will tell you on arrival if yours is one of them.
