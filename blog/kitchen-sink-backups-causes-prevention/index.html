    Kitchen Sink Backups: Causes, Prevention and Quick Solutions

    By Dana Whitfield · September 9, 2026 · 5 min read

    Why Kitchen Drains Back Up

    A kitchen sink usually drains through a 1-1/2 inch trap and arm into a 2-inch line inside the wall. For everything that goes down a kitchen drain, that is not much pipe. Grease goes down warm and liquid, cools a few feet into the wall, and sticks to the pipe, and every rinse of coffee grounds or rice after that catches on the coating until the opening is the size of a pencil. The sink drains slower for months, then stops.

    The Usual Suspects

    Grease and food are the big two. Disposals do not make food disappear, they just grind it smaller. Starchy things like pasta and potato peels swell and turn to paste, fibrous scraps like celery and onion skins wrap around the disposal and the trap, and coffee grounds settle like wet sand in any low spot. Eggshell bits act like grit in the paste.

    Sometimes the sink is fine and the problem is farther downstream. If the kitchen backs up when the washing machine drains or the dishwasher runs, the shared branch line is likely clogged. Listen for gurgling. A double sink that fills on the opposite side tells the same story.

    Older main lines that have shifted or sagged hold water in a low spot called a belly, and roots that find a cracked joint make the same spot worse. A camera tells us whether it is a belly, roots or just a grease plug. A cleaned belly fills up again within months.

    Rocky, shallow soil in hilly parts of Arkansas makes trenching harder and can add time on sewer and water line replacements.

    Prevention That Actually Works

    Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before they go in the sink. Pour cooled fat into a can and throw it out with the trash. Running hot water with grease does not help much, because the grease simply cools and sticks a little farther down the line where you cannot reach it, and dish soap only moves the problem along. Cold water is better with a disposal.

    A basket strainer costs about five dollars. Drop one in the drain and it stops most scraps before they reach the trap. Run the disposal for a few seconds after the grinding sound stops, scrape plates into the trash, and once a month fill the sink with hot water and pull the stopper so the volume pushes through the line. A monthly dose of bacterial drain treatment keeps the coating thin.

    Quick Fixes for a Slow or Stopped Sink

    Plunging It Right

    Use a flat-bottom sink plunger, not the flanged one from the toilet. Seal the second basin with a stopper or you will just push water back and forth. With a few inches of water in the sink, cover the drain fully and give it 15 to 20 firm strokes, pulling up as hard as you push down, since the suction stroke is what breaks a grease plug loose. Clamp the dishwasher hose first.

    Disposals have their own rules. Large batches of potato peels or pasta should go in the trash instead. A jammed disposal usually frees up with the hex wrench that fits the socket on the bottom of the unit, turned back and forth with the power off, followed by a press of the red reset button. Keep your hand out of the grinding chamber.

    Baking Soda, Vinegar and Hot Water

    This works on soft buildup, not a solid plug. Start with half a cup of baking soda, add a cup of white vinegar, then cover the drain for about 15 minutes. After that, flush with a kettle of hot water that is not quite boiling, since boiling water poured again and again can soften PVC joints and it does nothing for a clog sitting under standing water. Skip chemical drain openers. They can damage older pipe and splash back on whoever opens the trap next.

    When to Call a Professional

    Stop after two rounds of plunging. Once the sink refills or water shows up in another fixture, the clog is past what a plunger can reach. A tech from DGR Plumbing will pull the trap, run a cable or a small jetter through the wall line, and camera the main if more than one fixture is involved, and the number to reach us is (501) 251-9708. A typical kitchen drain cleaning runs less than an hour.

    Recurring clogs are a different problem. A sink that stops up every few weeks usually has a sagging line or heavy buildup deep in the wall. Hydro jetting scours the pipe back to bare wall, while a cable only punches a hole through the grease, and a camera inspection afterward shows whether the line itself needs repair. Sewage smell at the sink needs a look right away.
