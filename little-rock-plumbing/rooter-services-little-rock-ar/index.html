    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Little Rock

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet usually costs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout usually runs $200 to $500. Adding a jetting pass brings the total to about $450 to $1,000, depending on line length and how much grease is in it. Nights and weekends run higher.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. A cable can kink and wrap a hand or wrist fast. Rentals jam in pipes. Getting a rental cable stuck in clay pipe can mean digging it out, which costs far more than a rooter visit.

    Clearing a clog in Little Rock does not need a permit. Repairs are another matter. If the camera shows broken pipe that needs a dig, a new cleanout or a sewer replacement, a plumbing permit is usually required. We pull it and schedule the inspection for you.

    No permit is required for routine rooting. Installing a new cleanout, digging up a broken section or replacing the sewer lateral usually does require a plumbing permit and an inspection. We pull permits for any repair work we do.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go many years without it. A camera look can set the timing. Seeing how fast roots came back since the last cutting tells us whether to plan a yearly visit or stretch it to two years.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. A jetter scours the walls with water at high pressure. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.

    Watch for very slow drains, gurgling from toilets and sour odors. Water coming up in the shower when something else drains is a classic main line symptom. That one is urgent. Waiting raises the odds that the next flush puts sewage on the floor.
