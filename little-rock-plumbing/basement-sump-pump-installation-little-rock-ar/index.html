    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Little Rock

    Outages and heavy rain tend to show up together. Without house power, a battery backup keeps a second pump going, and a fully charged 75 amp-hour battery can run a typical backup pump for several hours of on-and-off cycling, longer on a light night. We test it under load.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Little Rock, AR

    Battery backups earn their keep during outages. A battery backup sump pump and a backwater valve are practical upgrades for homes that have taken on water before. We add a second pump in the same pit with its own float, wired to a sealed AGM battery on a smart charger that tests itself and beeps when the battery gets weak. Your primary pump still handles the everyday load.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Little Rock

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the water. That keeps it dry and easy to reach, which is why they suit narrow pits under 14 inches across, and why they often outlast cheap submersibles in Little Rock homes. Expect more noise.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Little Rock

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in Little Rock. The low end covers a pump swap in an existing pit, while a new pit cut through the slab, a long discharge run, or a battery backup pushes the total toward the top of that range. You get a written price after we look.

    Plan on 4 to 8 hours for a standard install. Digging a new pit and patching the slab can take the whole day, and tricky electrical access or a long discharge run through a finished wall adds time on top of that. You get a time estimate first.

    Permit rules vary by town, including Little Rock. Replacing a pump in the same pit rarely needs one, but a new pit, a new outlet or a new exit through the foundation wall often does, and we sort that out before any concrete gets cut. We handle the paperwork.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and a concrete patch takes most of a day, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Visible rust and a stuck float are other red flags, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Over seven years old is suspect.

    Labor on our sump pump installs is warrantied, typically for one year. The pump carries its own manufacturer warranty, usually one to five years depending on the brand and model, and we write the exact coverage on your invoice so you know which part to call about if something fails. Keep the receipt.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. A sticking float or rust streaks on the housing also mean trouble, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Do not wait for the storm.

    Mostly cast iron pumps from Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. All four have parts at local supply houses, which matters when a switch fails on a Sunday and you want the pump running again without waiting a week for an order to ship. We stock the common models.
