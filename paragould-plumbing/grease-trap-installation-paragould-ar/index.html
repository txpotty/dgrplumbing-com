    What Goes Into a Grease Trap Install in Paragould, AR

    Grease Trap Brands We Install in Paragould

    Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura cover most of what we set in commercial kitchens, plus precast concrete tanks for the big outdoor interceptors. The lid gaskets and baffles differ from brand to brand. Parts availability matters. We prefer models whose lids and gaskets are stocked nearby, because waiting a week on a part means waiting a week to reopen.

    Commercial Grease Trap Installation in Paragould

    A commercial kitchen with a three-compartment sink, a pre-rinse station and two floor sinks can push 50 gallons a minute of greasy water at the dinner rush. We size the trap to that peak flow, not the average. Undersized traps are the usual failure. We set the unit, run the inlet and outlet in schedule 40 PVC or cast iron, and add the flow control fitting the manufacturer calls for.

    Replacement and Upgrade Services in Paragould, AR

    Steel traps from decades back rust through at the baffle seams, and by then the outlet is usually passing grease straight to the sewer. A camera down the outlet line tells us how much damage the old unit already did. Sometimes the line needs jetting too. If your seats or menu grew since opening, the swap is the time to step up to a bigger GPM rating.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Paragould, AR

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. You can put one under a sink, beside a dish station or in a pit in the slab. The flow control is not optional. Without it, a dumped sink pushes water through faster than the unit is rated for and the grease goes out the outlet. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    Grease Trap Replacement and Upgrades in Paragould

    Plastic and steel traps both wear out, just in different ways. With steel, rust eats the seams and the welded baffles first. Plastic cracks. We remove the old unit, set the new one in the same spot when it fits, and use no-hub couplings so future work is easier.

    Gravity Grease Interceptor Installation in Paragould, AR

    Concrete or polyethylene, a buried gravity interceptor is a two-compartment tank with tees on the inlet and outlet that reach down below the grease layer. Concrete is heavier and holds up under traffic. Poly is easier to set. With a plastic tank in wet ground we add anti-flotation anchoring, because an empty tank in saturated soil can pop right out of the hole.

    Gravity Grease Trap Installation in Paragould

    A gravity interceptor works on time, not machinery. The tank holds water for roughly half an hour, which gives grease time to rise and food solids time to drop. Sizing comes from your fixture units and the retention time your sewer authority wants, and we check that number with them before ordering a tank. No moving parts to fail.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Paragould

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. The air intake has to be vented to code, not left open in the kitchen. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. A trap that sits out of level loses capacity, so we shim it flat before connecting anything.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Paragould

We give you a written estimate after we walk the site, and it does not change unless we hit something underground nobody could see.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. You get copies of every approval for your files. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. For a small indoor trap in a busy kitchen, that can be weekly. Outdoor tanks go longer. A gravity interceptor typically gets pumped monthly to quarterly, and some sewer programs set the schedule for you.

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. For fry-heavy kitchens we also set automatic units that skim grease on a timer. Most cafes go hydromechanical. Big-volume kitchens and anything the local FOG program flags as high risk usually end up with a gravity interceptor outside the building.

    Two warranties apply here. The trap body carries the maker's coverage, which runs from one year on some steel units to much longer on plastic. We cover our workmanship, meaning leaks at our joints and settling of a tank we set, and we give you the terms in writing before we start.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. We like the plastic bodies from Schier where steel would rust out. Brand matters less than sizing. A well-known trap installed too small will still fail inspection, so we pick the size first and the brand second.
