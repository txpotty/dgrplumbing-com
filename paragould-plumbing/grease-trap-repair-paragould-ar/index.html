    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Repair in Paragould

    The cost of grease trap repair in Paragould varies widely depending on the nature and severity of the problem. Minor clogs might range from $200 to $400, while more extensive repairs like replacing baffles or fixing large leaks could be $500 to $1,500 or more. We provide a detailed estimate after a thorough inspection.

    Minor repairs like clearing a clog or replacing a gasket usually do not require a permit. However, significant structural repairs, replacing major components, or any work that alters the original plumbing configuration of the grease trap might require a permit from the local Paragould building department. We will advise you if a permit is needed.

    Many problems such as broken tees, worn gaskets and damaged lids can be repaired. Cracked tank bodies or badly corroded units typically need replacement.

    Key signs include slow drainage in your kitchen sinks, foul odors coming from drains or the trap itself, visible FOGs in the wastewater leaving the trap, or an overflow. If you notice any of these, it's time to call a professional for an inspection to prevent further damage or violations.

    Most indoor repairs take a few hours. Outdoor interceptor repairs may take a day or more, especially if pumping, excavation or concrete work is needed.

    Persistent odors, grease in downstream lines, leaks around the trap, slow drains and broken lids are common signs. If cleaning does not fix the problem, a repair is likely needed.

    Grease traps are smaller, typically located inside or near the kitchen, and are suitable for low-volume establishments. Grease interceptors are much larger, often installed underground outside the building, and are designed to handle the high FOG volumes from large restaurants and commercial facilities. Both serve the same purpose of preventing FOGs from entering the sewer system.

    The primary cause is the accumulation of FOG (Fats, Oils, and Greases) which solidifies and creates blockages. Other causes include structural damage from age or impact, corrosion of internal components, improper installation, or lack of regular maintenance and cleaning.
