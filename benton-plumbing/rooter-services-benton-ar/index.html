    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Benton

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet usually costs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. With hydro jetting added, expect roughly $450 to $1,000, depending on how long the line is and how heavy the grease is. Nights and weekends run higher.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go years without it. A camera look can set the timing. How quickly roots return after the last cut tells us if yearly or every-other-year service fits your line.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. They can soften old PVC glue joints and pit cast iron over time. They also burn skin. A cable takes the clog out instead of melting a narrow path through it, which keeps the drain open longer.

    Rooting is safe with a trained tech and the correct cable and blade. Collapsed or badly cracked pipe can be hurt by anything run through it. Feel matters. If the cable tells us the pipe is damaged, we stop and send in a camera before going further.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. Both brands hold up to daily use. We keep cables and cutter heads in several sizes on board, which lets the tech match the tool to your pipe instead of forcing one setup on every drain.

    No permit is needed for routine rooting. A new cleanout or a pipe repair may need one. We pull permits for any repair work we do.

    Watch for very slow drains, gurgling from toilets and sour odors. Water coming up in the shower when something else drains is a classic main line symptom. That one is urgent. The longer it goes, the more likely the next flush ends with sewage on the bathroom floor.

    A limited guarantee for a set number of days comes with most rooter work. If the line clogs again within that time, we return at no extra charge.
