    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Pipe Repair in Rogers

    Expect roughly $80 to $200 per linear foot. Bigger pipe costs more. That can look higher than digging at first, but trenchless usually saves money once you count the landscaping, concrete and irrigation an open trench would destroy, and the full estimate comes after an inspection.

    Compared to digging, yes. Far less dirt is disturbed. Trenchless repair leaves trees, plants and soil in place, and because the old pipe usually stays in the ground as the host, very little material ends up hauled to a landfill.

    Trenchless repair takes specialized equipment, materials and training. It should be done by an experienced contractor.

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. Bursting replaces it. With CIPP, a resin-soaked liner cures against the old pipe wall, while bursting breaks the old pipe apart and pulls new HDPE into its place, leaving a completely new pipe.

    Cast iron, clay, PVC and concrete can all be lined. The pipe's shape counts. If the pipe has collapsed or shifted badly, bursting may suit it better than lining, and the camera tells us which.

    CIPP liners and resins from Perma-Liner. Prep is done with Picote cutters. Both are proven in sewer work, and every material we install meets or exceeds local plumbing code for strength and durability, which matters for a pipe expected to last decades.

    Lining reduces the inside diameter slightly, but the smooth surface often improves flow. Pipe bursting can maintain or increase the diameter.

    Most cracked, rooted or separated pipes can be lined. Collapses are different. A fully collapsed or deeply sagging line may need bursting or a dig, and the camera shows us which.
