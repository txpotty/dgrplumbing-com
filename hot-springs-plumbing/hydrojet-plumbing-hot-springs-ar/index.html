    Hydrojetting vs Snaking Cost Comparison in Hot Springs, AR

    Snaking a main line typically costs $200 to $450, while residential hydrojetting usually runs $400 to $900 with a camera inspection. The jet is the bigger bill. Fewer repeat visits often make jetting cheaper over a few years.

    Root Removal Jetting in Hot Springs

    Shade trees and shrubs send roots toward small cracks in clay and cast iron pipe. Root-cutting nozzles clear them. Roots come back through the same cracks, so the camera shows where a repair or liner would stop them.

    Whole-House Drain Jetting in Hot Springs

    Slow drains in several rooms point to buildup in the main or a branch line. The main gets jetted from the cleanout, and branches from vents or fixtures. Plan on two to three hours.

    Sewer Camera Inspection Before Hydrojetting in Hot Springs

    Jetting a broken pipe can make the break worse. We camera every line before we set up the jetter. The video shows the pipe material, the condition of each joint, and any cracks, offsets or sags, which tells us the nozzle and pressure to use or whether we should not jet that line at all.

    Emergency Hydrojet Services in Hot Springs, AR

    A full sewer backup needs a truck today. We clear it first. A cable opens it, then the jetter removes the grease, roots or wipes that caused it.

    Hydrojetting for Cast Iron Pipes in Hot Springs, AR

    Older cast iron drains rust and scale from the inside, leaving a rough surface that grabs debris. Jetting can remove that buildup. Badly corroded cast iron with thin walls or cracks needs gentler settings or no jetting at all, and the camera tells us which before we start, along with whether the pipe is a candidate for lining.

    Main Sewer Line Hydrojetting in Hot Springs

    The main sewer carries every drain in the house, so a blockage there backs up the lowest fixture first. Jetting the main clears roots, grease and sludge all the way to the city connection. On a 4-inch line we use a heavier nozzle with more flow, which moves the loosened debris downstream instead of leaving it to settle in a low spot.
