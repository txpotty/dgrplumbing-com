    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Hot Springs, AR

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. For a typical house, copper often runs 20 to 40 percent above PEX, though some buyers and homeowners like the look and feel of metal pipe and are willing to pay for it. Our quotes for either include permit, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Hot Springs

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. We can reroute a single hot or cold branch overhead and abandon the leaking pipe in the slab, which costs far less than a repipe and avoids breaking the floor. This works well for a first leak. After multiple leaks, we will show you why a full repipe may cost less over the next ten years.

    Copper Repiping in Hot Springs, AR

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. We install Type L copper, which has a thicker wall than the Type M used in many older homes, with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings. Copper takes a bit longer. Most copper jobs run a day longer than PEX, since each turn needs its own fitting.

    Whole-Home Copper Repiping in Hot Springs

    Copper is the traditional choice, and Type L copper has a thicker wall than the Type M that went into many older homes. Type L tolerates UV, stays rigid and has decades of field history. It costs more. Our plumbers solder joints with lead-free solder or use press fittings, support the pipe at the spacing the code calls for and pressure test the system before any wall is closed.

    Repiping for Hard Water in Hot Springs, AR

    Rural wells in Arkansas often carry iron, sediment, or a sulfur odor, which can stain fixtures and foul aerators and water heaters. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. In hard water homes, PEX-A is often the better pick since it resists scale, and a softener loop or filter can be added during the job. Adding the loop now costs less than later.

    Urgent Repipes After Repeated Failures in Hot Springs, AR

    A widespread pipe failure sometimes forces a repipe sooner than you planned. The first step is temporary repairs so you have water, and then we book the repipe as quickly as the permit and schedule allow. We move fast. Even urgent repipes usually take just two to four days after we begin.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Hot Springs

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Both still turn up in older homes. The pipe at the water heater usually tells us which one you have, and we replace the full system so the home is easier to insure and to sell.
