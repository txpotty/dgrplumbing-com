    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Hot Springs

    We offer installation for both PEX-A and PEX-B piping and understand the distinct advantages of each. PEX-A, often preferred for its flexibility and ability to recover from minor kinks, uses expansion fittings. PEX-B is more rigid but also very durable, usually using crimp or compression fittings. Our experts in Hot Springs can help you decide which type of PEX suits your project, considering factors like budget and application.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Hot Springs

    Copper lines can develop pinhole leaks from water chemistry, high velocity and sometimes soil contact under slabs. Rural wells in Arkansas often carry iron, sediment, or a sulfur odor, which can stain fixtures and foul aerators and water heaters. Once a home has had several pinhole leaks, more usually follow. Converting to PEX stops the cycle and reduces the risk of hidden water damage. We use approved transition fittings wherever new PEX connects to existing copper stubs or valves.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Hot Springs, AR

    PEX-A, such as Uponor, uses an expansion tool to stretch the pipe and a ring over the fitting, which then shrinks back into a secure seal. The fittings have a larger inner diameter, so flow restriction is lower. PEX-A is also more flexible and can recover from kinks with a heat gun. It is our preferred choice for many full repipes.

    PEX is a good fit for a wide range of climates because of its flexibility and resistance to freezing. Big temperature swings between seasons expand and contract pipes and fittings, which can loosen older joints over time. While rigid pipes can burst when water freezes, PEX can expand and contract, significantly reducing the risk of burst pipes during cold snaps. It also performs well in high temperatures, making it a reliable choice year-round in Hot Springs.

    Signs you might need PEX repiping include consistently low water pressure, discolored or rusty water, frequent leaks, or old galvanized steel or polybutylene pipes. These issues are typical in older Hot Springs homes and point to a failing plumbing system.
