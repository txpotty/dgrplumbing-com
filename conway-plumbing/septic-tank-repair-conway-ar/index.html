    Septic Alarm Troubleshooting in Conway

    A septic alarm sounding is the system's way of saying the water is too high in the pump chamber or the aerobic unit. Press the silence button and cut back on water. Our tech confirms the pump runs and looks for a float snagged on its cord. A tripped breaker is the cheapest answer.

    House-to-Tank Line Repair in Conway

    Gurgling drains right after a pump-out point to the line between the house and the tank, not the tank itself. Roots and settling ground cause most sags and breaks in that run. A camera from the house cleanout finds the bad joint, and we mark it in the yard. Then we dig one hole, not a trench. A second cleanout near the tank goes in while it is open.

    Leach Field Restoration in Conway, AR

    Before we condemn a leach field, we open the distribution box and look at how evenly it splits the flow. A box that has settled or cracked can push all the effluent into a single trench. A new or leveled box brings a lot of fields back. Some fields are past saving. For those, we explain the design and permit steps.

    Septic Pump and Alarm Repair in Conway

    Pumped systems move effluent uphill or to a far field, and the pump sits in its own chamber with a float tree and an alarm. An alarm going off means the water rose past the high-level float. We check the breaker and meter the pump draw before replacing anything. Zoeller and Liberty parts ride on the truck.

    Commercial Septic Repair in Conway, AR

    Churches and small offices on septic run more water through the tank than any house, and a commercial kitchen adds grease on top of that. Repairs get sized to real daily flow instead of house numbers. Grease interceptors and lift stations are part of it. Downtime costs you, so we schedule around open hours.

    Inlet and Outlet Baffle Replacement in Conway, AR

    Baffles hold back the scum layer on top and the sludge on the bottom so only clear effluent leaves the tank. Concrete ones get eaten by sulfuric acid from sewer gas and eventually fall off into the tank. Once the outlet baffle is gone, solids head straight for the field. We replace them with PVC sanitary tees. Usually a short job.

    Septic Tee Repair in Conway

    Sanitary tees on the inlet and outlet crack or pop loose, sometimes from a careless pump-out hose. Without the outlet tee, the floating scum layer flows straight into the field lines and seals the trenches. We glue in a new Schedule 40 tee. An effluent filter goes on if the outlet has room.

    Septic Code and Permit Work in Conway, AR

    New tanks and new fields need a permit from the local health authority in most of the country, along with any change to the system's layout. We draw the site plan and file the paperwork, then schedule the inspection before the trench is backfilled. Small fixes like a tee usually skip the permit. The inspector's sign-off goes in your file.
